# WildKind Pet Personality Assessment Program

**Version:** Product and research specification v0.1  
**Initial species:** Companion dogs and companion cats  
**Primary purpose:** Help owners observe recurring behavioral tendencies, select appropriate enrichment and care strategies, and communicate with owners of behaviorally similar pets.  
**Not intended for:** Veterinary diagnosis, aggression prediction, adoption clearance, breeding selection, service-animal selection, or certification that two animals can interact safely.

---

## 1. Product definition

### 1.1 Positioning statement

WildKind is a species-aware behavioral personality profile that combines structured owner observations with optional, low-stress behavioral tasks. It measures five continuous behavioral domains and translates the profile into a narrative archetype and individualized care suggestions.

The scientific result is the five-dimensional profile. The archetype is a communication and storytelling layer; it is not a separate psychological diagnosis or a fixed biological type.

### 1.2 Scientific claim boundary

WildKind is informed by comparative animal-personality research, but it does not claim that the human Big Five appears unchanged in every species. Cross-species research has found stronger generality for some human-like dimensions than others; in particular, a separate Conscientiousness dimension is not consistently recovered across nonhuman species. Cat research has also produced a species-specific factor structure rather than the human Big Five.

Therefore:

- Measurement items must be validated separately for dogs and cats.
- Scores from different species must never be placed in the same normative population.
- Human moral or cognitive interpretations must not be inferred from animal behavior.
- The terms "validated," "percentile," and "confidence interval" may be used only after the required empirical work is complete.

### 1.3 WildKind's five domains

| Code | Public name | Operational definition | What it does not mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| DD | Discovery Drive | Tendency to approach, investigate, and persist with safe novelty | Intelligence, courage, or learning capacity |
| SE | Social Energy | Frequency and intensity of voluntary social participation | Popularity, kindness, or social competence |
| BS | Bonding Style | Tendency toward affiliation, cooperative proximity, and comfortable familiar contact | Love, loyalty, or moral goodness |
| BR | Behavioral Regulation | Tendency to pause, disengage, transition, and recover from blocked access | Human conscientiousness, obedience, or being "well behaved" |
| ER | Emotional Resilience | Relative intensity and duration of everyday stress responses, expressed positively as recovery capacity | Absence of fear, a clinical anxiety diagnosis, or guaranteed safety |

For research storage, ER should also be retained as its unreversed construct, **Stress Reactivity (SR = 100 − ER)**. This reduces analytic errors and makes the direction of behavioral indicators explicit.

## 2. Assessment products

### 2.1 WildKind Snapshot

An approximately five-minute online screening completed by an adult owner or primary caregiver.

It produces:

- Five owner-reported domain scores
- A data-coverage indicator
- One provisional narrative archetype
- Three dimension-based care ideas
- A retest recommendation
- Welfare guidance when concerning changes are reported

It must be labeled **Owner-Reported WildKind Snapshot**.

### 2.2 WildKind Enhanced Profile

A 20–30 minute structured assessment completed in a familiar or carefully controlled environment by a trained assessor, in addition to the owner questionnaire.

It produces:

- The complete Snapshot report
- Five observed-session domain indicators
- Questionnaire–observation comparison
- Context discrepancy notes
- Assessor notes based on the behavior codebook
- A more detailed care plan

Until empirical integration weights have been validated, the Enhanced Profile must not collapse owner and observed data into a single supposedly more accurate number. Both sources should remain visible.

### 2.3 WildKind Journey

A longitudinal view generated after two or more valid assessments.

It produces:

- Raw scores over time
- Changes in the pet's environment and health
- Data-source changes
- Age-adjusted comparisons only after age norms exist
- A prompt to distinguish persistent tendencies from temporary state changes

## 3. Eligibility, context, and welfare rules

### 3.1 Snapshot eligibility

The owner may complete the questionnaire if:

- The pet has lived with the respondent for at least 30 days.
- The respondent has regular contact with the pet.
- The respondent is at least the minimum legal age required by the product's jurisdiction.
- The pet is not currently experiencing an obvious medical emergency.

Results should be marked **emerging** when:

- The pet has lived with the respondent for 30–89 days.
- The animal is in a major adjustment period.
- Fewer than four items can be scored in any domain.
- The owner reports a recent major health, medication, household, or environment change.

### 3.2 Enhanced-assessment exclusions

Do not begin or continue structured tasks when the animal:

- Shows signs of pain, breathing difficulty, collapse, repeated vomiting, disorientation, or acute illness.
- Has a known history that makes the planned situation unsafe.
- Is recovering from surgery or an acute medical event.
- Is being tested in a setting where it cannot voluntarily retreat.
- Is already showing sustained severe distress before the assessment begins.

### 3.3 Universal stop rules

Stop the current task immediately if any of the following occurs:

- Repeated escape attempts or frantic exit-focused behavior
- Freezing that persists for 20 seconds without softening
- Sustained trembling, crouching, or hiding with no voluntary re-engagement
- Growling, snarling, hissing, lunging, swatting, or defensive striking directed at a person or animal
- Self-injurious behavior
- Continuous high-intensity vocalization for 20 seconds
- Loss of bladder or bowel control apparently associated with distress
- The owner or assessor requests that the task stop

After a stop event, create a factual note, do not repeat the task, do not assign a low personality score from the incomplete task, and offer appropriate veterinary or behavior-professional guidance.

### 3.4 Language rules

WildKind reports should say:

- "showed," "approached," "withdrew," "recovered," and "was observed"
- "may prefer" and "may benefit from"
- "in this setting" and "during the recent observation period"

They should not say:

- "is aggressive," "is anxious," "is dominant," or "is disordered"
- "will get along with"
- "is smarter," "more loyal," or "better behaved"
- "the algorithm knows"

## 4. Owner questionnaire administration

### 4.1 Instructions shown to owners

> Think about your pet's behavior during the last 30 days. Choose the answer that best reflects what happened across several real occasions, not one unusually good or difficult day. If the situation did not occur or you could not observe it, choose “Not observed.” There are no good or bad personality scores.

### 4.2 Scored response scale

| Response | Score | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Never | 0 | The behavior did not occur in relevant observed situations |
| Rarely | 1 | It occurred occasionally but was unusual |
| Sometimes | 2 | It occurred in roughly half of relevant situations or varied substantially |
| Often | 3 | It occurred in most relevant situations |
| Almost always | 4 | It occurred consistently across nearly all relevant situations |
| Not observed / not applicable | Missing | The situation did not occur or the owner could not judge it |

Do not force a score for “Not observed.” Missingness is information about coverage, not evidence of an average trait.

### 4.3 Species-aware wording

The website should insert species-appropriate examples without changing the scored construct.

- Dog examples may include walks, harnessing, toys, visitors, sniffing, and familiar compatible dogs.
- Cat examples may include room access, carriers, play objects, visitors, household sounds, elevated resting places, and familiar compatible cats.
- Contact questions must refer only to contact the individual animal normally accepts. Picking up a cat or touching paws must never be treated as universally routine.

## 5. Complete 32-item WildKind Snapshot

Items should appear in a mixed order in production. Domain codes and scoring directions must be hidden from the user.

### 5.1 Discovery Drive — five scored items

1. **Safe new object:** When a safe unfamiliar object appeared in a familiar space, my pet voluntarily moved closer to inspect it within about two minutes. **[DD+]**
2. **New accessible space:** When given free access to an unfamiliar room or area, my pet moved beyond the entrance and investigated. **[DD+]**
3. **Changed familiar environment:** When a familiar object was moved or the room arrangement changed, my pet checked the changed area without being led to it. **[DD+]**
4. **New enrichment:** When offered a safe new puzzle, toy, scent source, or search activity, my pet continued investigating after the first contact. **[DD+]**
5. **Continued avoidance:** After noticing a harmless unfamiliar object from a safe distance, my pet continued avoiding it for the remainder of the observation. **[DD−]**

### 5.2 Social Energy — five scored items

6. **Familiar-person initiation:** While awake and free to choose, my pet initiated play, proximity, or another social interaction with a familiar person. **[SE+]**
7. **Joining activity:** When familiar household members gathered for an ordinary activity, my pet voluntarily entered or remained in the shared area. **[SE+]**
8. **Sustained engagement:** After voluntarily beginning a social interaction, my pet remained engaged or reinitiated it for at least two minutes. **[SE+]**
9. **Compatible-animal interaction:** When a known, compatible animal was available, my pet initiated or willingly joined a non-threatening interaction. Select “Not observed” if there was no safe opportunity. **[SE+]**
10. **Declining familiar invitations:** When a familiar person calmly invited interaction and my pet was free to approach, my pet remained apart or moved farther away. **[SE−]**

### 5.3 Bonding Style — five scored items

11. **Comfortable familiar contact:** During a form of gentle contact this pet normally accepts, its body remained loose and it stayed voluntarily. **[BS+]**
12. **Re-engagement:** If familiar contact paused while the pet was still interested, my pet moved closer, remained nearby, or otherwise invited the interaction to continue. **[BS+]**
13. **Cooperative routine:** During a familiar, low-stress care routine, my pet remained available and could complete the routine without being restrained beyond its normal practice. **[BS+]**
14. **Shared neutral space:** In a resource-neutral situation, my pet comfortably shared space with a familiar person or known compatible animal. **[BS+]**
15. **Tension during familiar approach:** When a familiar caregiver approached calmly during an ordinary neutral moment, my pet showed tension or withdrew before contact was attempted. **[BS−]**

### 5.4 Behavioral Regulation — five scored items

16. **Blocked access:** When access to a desired but nonessential item or place was briefly blocked, my pet could pause or move to another activity without repeated escalation. **[BR+]**
17. **Activity ending:** After an ordinary play or enrichment session ended, my pet shifted to another activity or began settling within about five minutes. **[BR+]**
18. **Available alternative:** When the preferred option was unavailable, my pet engaged with an available alternative rather than repeatedly attempting only the blocked option. **[BR+]**
19. **Routine transition:** During a familiar daily transition, such as leaving a play area or preparing for rest, my pet completed the transition without repeated prompting or physical pressure. **[BR+]**
20. **Escalating repetition:** When a desired outcome did not occur, my pet repeated the same action with increasing intensity even after the opportunity had clearly ended. **[BR−]**

### 5.5 Emotional Resilience — five scored items

21. **Ordinary unexpected sound:** After an ordinary unexpected household sound, my pet resumed its previous activity or another relaxed activity within about two minutes. **[ER+]**
22. **Visitor or routine change:** Following a manageable visitor arrival or minor routine change, my pet returned to its usual behavior within about ten minutes. **[ER+]**
23. **Brief frustration recovery:** After a minor everyday frustration, my pet's body and activity returned to its usual state within about five minutes. **[ER+]**
24. **Caregiver briefly out of view:** During normal household movement when a familiar caregiver was briefly out of view, my pet remained able to rest, explore, eat, or engage in another ordinary activity. **[ER+]**
25. **Persistent response:** After a mild trigger had ended, signs such as vigilant scanning, hiding, pacing, trembling, or exit focus continued for more than ten minutes. **[ER−]**

### 5.6 Context and data-quality items — seven unscored items

26. **Time together:** How long has this pet lived with you?
   - Less than 30 days
   - 30–89 days
   - 3–12 months
   - More than 12 months

27. **Health change:** During the last 30 days, has your pet had pain, illness, surgery, injury, reduced mobility, or a notable appetite or sleep change?
   - No known change
   - Possible minor change
   - Confirmed or substantial change
   - Unsure

28. **Medication change:** During the last 30 days, was a medication started, stopped, or substantially changed?
   - No
   - Yes
   - Unsure

29. **Life change:** During the last 30 days, did the pet experience a move, new household member, loss of a companion, new animal, boarding, adoption, major schedule change, or frightening event?
   - No major change
   - One manageable change
   - One major or several changes
   - Prefer not to say

30. **Sudden behavioral change:** Compared with the previous three months, has any behavior changed suddenly or markedly?
   - No
   - Yes, but it has resolved
   - Yes, and it is continuing
   - I have not known the pet long enough

31. **Observation coverage:** In how many of the 25 situations above did you recall at least three relevant occasions?
   - 0–9
   - 10–15
   - 16–20
   - 21–25

32. **Independent perspective:** Has another regular caregiver independently described this pet's recent behavior?
   - No second observer is available
   - Yes, and our observations are mostly similar
   - Yes, and our observations differ in several areas
   - I am not sure

### 5.7 Safety follow-up prompts

If item 27, 28, or 30 indicates a significant or ongoing change, show:

> Personality-style scores can be affected by pain, illness, medication, and major environmental changes. A sudden behavior change deserves veterinary attention before it is interpreted as personality.

If the owner reports severe separation behavior, self-injury, repeated defensive behavior, or inability to recover, provide a separate welfare pathway. Do not turn those signals into a playful archetype description.

## 6. Questionnaire scoring

### 6.1 Reverse scoring

Reverse-score items 5, 10, 15, 20, and 25:

`reversed score = 4 − selected score`

### 6.2 Domain scores

For each domain:

`domain mean = sum of available keyed item scores / number of available items`

`domain score = domain mean × 25`

This produces a provisional score from 0 to 100.

Calculate a domain only when at least four of its five items are answered. Otherwise show **Insufficient observation**.

### 6.3 Coverage status

| Condition | Coverage label |
|---|---|
| All five domain items answered and item 31 indicates strong opportunity coverage | Strong coverage |
| Four domain items answered or item 31 indicates moderate opportunity coverage | Adequate coverage |
| Fewer than four domain items answered | Insufficient observation |

Coverage is not psychometric confidence. It describes only whether the owner had enough opportunities to answer.

### 6.4 Context flags

Create non-diagnostic flags:

- `NEW_HOME`: living together for less than 90 days
- `HEALTH_CONTEXT`: health or mobility change reported
- `MEDICATION_CONTEXT`: medication change reported
- `LIFE_CHANGE`: major recent environmental change
- `SUDDEN_CHANGE`: continuing sudden behavior change
- `OBSERVER_DIFFERENCE`: regular caregivers report different patterns

Flags modify report wording and retest timing; they must not mechanically add or subtract trait points.

### 6.5 Prohibited output before norming

Before species- and life-stage-specific norms are validated, do not show:

- Population percentiles
- “Higher than 73% of pets” claims
- Numerical confidence intervals
- Clinical thresholds
- Claims that a score is high or low relative to a population

During the pilot phase, scores may be described within the profile using neutral bands:

- 0–34: less frequently expressed in this assessment
- 35–65: context-dependent or moderate expression
- 66–100: more frequently expressed in this assessment

These are product-description bands, not population norms.

## 7. Enhanced observation protocol

### 7.1 Setting

Cats should normally be assessed in their home or another genuinely familiar environment. Dogs may be assessed at home or in a quiet assessment room after an acclimation period.

Minimum setup:

- Non-slip floor
- Voluntary retreat area
- Water available
- No unfamiliar animal present
- No food deprivation requirement
- Owner present unless an optional, separately consented module requires otherwise
- Two fixed camera views when recording is consented to
- Session event log synchronized with video time

### 7.2 Assessor training

Assessors must demonstrate:

- Recognition of species-specific stress, affiliative, defensive, and displacement behavior
- Accurate use of the codebook
- Ability to apply stop rules without waiting for owner permission
- Inter-rater agreement against certification videos
- Understanding that task noncompletion is missing data, not failure

### 7.3 Session sequence

1. Health and context check — 2 minutes
2. Free acclimation and baseline — 3 minutes minimum
3. Task 1: Safe novelty — up to 4 minutes
4. Recovery break — at least 2 minutes
5. Task 2: Neutral unfamiliar person — up to 4 minutes
6. Recovery break — at least 2 minutes
7. Task 3: Familiar cooperative routine — up to 3 minutes
8. Task 4: Brief blocked-access and alternative-choice task — up to 3 minutes
9. Recovery break — at least 2 minutes
10. Task 5: Mild environmental change and recovery — up to 3 minutes
11. Free recovery and session close — 3 minutes

The order of Tasks 1–5 should eventually be tested for order effects. In v0.1, use one documented order consistently and record any deviation.

### 7.4 Baseline

The owner behaves normally but does not repeatedly call or lure the animal. Record:

- Location every 15 seconds
- Activity state
- Voluntary proximity to owner
- Social initiations
- Body-tension indicators
- Exploration
- Whether a stable baseline is reached

Do not begin tasks until the animal shows at least 60 seconds without escalating distress. If that does not occur, end the assessment.

### 7.5 Task 1 — Safe novelty

**Purpose:** Observe voluntary investigation and recovery around harmless novelty.

**Procedure:**

1. With the animal able to retreat, place one safe, species-appropriate unfamiliar object at a standard distance.
2. The owner and assessor remain neutral for 60 seconds.
3. If the animal is relaxed, the owner may interact naturally with the object once, without pushing it toward the animal.
4. End after four minutes, earlier if the animal loses interest, or immediately under a stop rule.

**Do not use:** motorized objects, startling sounds, strong scents, food-shaped hazards, unstable surfaces, or objects resembling known fear triggers.

**Record:** orientation latency, approach latency, minimum distance, contact count, interaction duration, diversity of investigation, withdrawal, and time to resume baseline activity.

### 7.6 Task 2 — Neutral unfamiliar person

**Purpose:** Observe voluntary social approach, affiliation, avoidance, and recovery.

**Procedure:**

1. An unfamiliar person enters or becomes visible at a standard distance.
2. The person turns slightly sideways, avoids direct staring, remains quiet, and does not reach toward the animal.
3. After 60 seconds, if the animal remains relaxed, the person may speak softly once.
4. Touch occurs only if the animal clearly initiates and the species-specific protocol permits it.
5. End after four minutes or under a stop rule.

**Record:** orientation, approach initiation, minimum distance, time spent within the proximity zone, affiliative signals, withdrawal, tension, and recovery after the person leaves.

This task must never be interpreted as a definitive stranger-safety test.

### 7.7 Task 3 — Familiar cooperative routine

**Purpose:** Observe affiliation and regulation during an already familiar, low-stress interaction.

The owner selects one routine the pet normally accepts, such as presenting a harness, moving onto a familiar mat, gentle brushing, entering a familiar carrier voluntarily, or another pre-screened activity.

**Procedure:**

1. Confirm the routine is familiar and not currently associated with pain.
2. Owner gives the normal invitation once.
3. No forced positioning or restraint is permitted.
4. Pause after the first successful step and observe whether the animal remains, disengages, or re-engages.
5. End within three minutes.

**Record:** response latency, voluntary participation, number of prompts, body looseness, withdrawal, re-engagement, and transition after the routine ends.

Untrained or unfamiliar routines are ineligible and must be coded missing.

### 7.8 Task 4 — Brief blocked access with an available alternative

**Purpose:** Observe persistence, flexibility, and recovery without inducing prolonged frustration.

**Procedure:**

1. Confirm the animal is interested in a safe toy, enrichment item, or low-value food reward appropriate to its care plan.
2. Allow one successful interaction.
3. Make the same target visible but inaccessible for no more than 30 seconds.
4. Place an accessible alternative nearby.
5. Restore access or end immediately if frustration escalates.

**Record:** attempts toward blocked target, escalation, pause, alternative engagement, strategy changes, orientation to owner, and recovery.

Do not use food if dietary restriction, guarding history, or lack of food motivation makes it inappropriate.

### 7.9 Task 5 — Mild environmental change

**Purpose:** Observe response and recovery after a non-startling, reversible change.

Examples include placing a neutral mat in a familiar route or moving a familiar lightweight object to a new visible position. The change must not block escape or essential resources.

**Procedure:**

1. Introduce the pre-approved change while the animal can observe from a comfortable distance.
2. Do not produce a sudden loud noise.
3. Observe for up to two minutes.
4. Restore the environment and observe recovery for one minute.

**Record:** orientation latency, investigation, route adjustment, avoidance, body tension, and recovery.

### 7.10 Optional owner-absence module

Brief owner absence is not a core personality task. It may be used only in a separately validated welfare protocol with explicit consent, remote monitoring, strict stop criteria, and veterinary-behavior oversight. It must not be called a separation-anxiety test, and one session cannot establish a diagnosis.

## 8. Observation coding

### 8.1 General 0–4 coding anchors

Each observable metric must have a task-specific codebook. The following general direction may guide development:

| Code | Behavioral expression |
|---:|---|
| 0 | No expression or sustained opposite behavior |
| 1 | Brief or low-intensity expression |
| 2 | Mixed, delayed, or context-dependent expression |
| 3 | Clear repeated or sustained expression |
| 4 | Rapid, frequent, or sustained expression without prompting |

Do not use this generic scale where exact latency, duration, distance, or frequency can be retained. Store raw observations first; derived 0–4 codes are secondary.

### 8.2 Required raw metrics

| Metric | Unit |
|---|---|
| Orientation latency | Seconds |
| Approach latency | Seconds; censored if no approach |
| Minimum distance | Centimeters or body lengths |
| Interaction duration | Seconds |
| Contact or attempt count | Count |
| Prompt count | Count |
| Recovery time | Seconds; censored if no recovery during task |
| Location sampling | Zone every 15 seconds |
| Task completion | Complete / partial / stopped / not applicable |

### 8.3 Provisional observed-domain composition

These mappings are hypotheses for pilot testing, not final weights:

- **Observed DD:** novelty approach, novelty contact, investigation diversity, strategy variation, investigation of environmental change
- **Observed SE:** baseline social initiation, unfamiliar-person approach, voluntary proximity, sustained social engagement
- **Observed BS:** comfortable familiar contact, voluntary cooperative participation, remaining nearby, re-engagement after pause
- **Observed BR:** pause after blocked access, use of alternative, transition after activity, number of prompts, de-escalation
- **Observed ER:** intensity and recovery metrics across novelty, unfamiliar-person, blocked-access, and environmental-change tasks

For v0.1, report the observed indicators descriptively and display their task coverage. Do not combine them using unvalidated weights.

### 8.4 Inter-rater quality rule

At least 20% of pilot videos should be independently coded by a second blinded assessor. Metrics that cannot reach acceptable agreement after codebook revision should not contribute to a score.

## 9. Questionnaire–observation comparison

### 9.1 Comparison status

For each dimension, show one of four states:

- **Similar pattern:** the two sources are directionally close
- **Context variation:** the sources differ beyond the provisional comparison band
- **Not comparable:** one source lacks enough valid data
- **Research pending:** integration thresholds have not yet been validated

In the pre-validation product, “context variation” may be displayed only as a descriptive comparison, not as a statistical anomaly.

### 9.2 Approved discrepancy message

> Your description and today's observation differed in this area. Behavior often changes with setting, familiarity, health, recent events, and available opportunities. WildKind keeps both observations visible rather than treating either one as automatically correct.

### 9.3 Prohibited discrepancy message

Do not say:

- "The owner is biased."
- "AI detected an inaccurate answer."
- "The real personality is..."
- "The observation overrules the questionnaire."

## 10. Confidence and evidence labels

WildKind should use three separate concepts.

### 10.1 Coverage

Whether enough relevant situations were observed to calculate a score.

### 10.2 Consistency

After validation, whether items, occasions, or observers produce sufficiently similar measurements. This requires empirical reliability estimates.

### 10.3 Context stability

Whether major health or environmental factors make a stable-trait interpretation less appropriate.

Recommended report labels:

- **Emerging profile:** limited history, limited coverage, or major recent context change
- **Developing profile:** adequate owner observations with some contextual uncertainty
- **Established profile:** adequate longitudinal or multi-source evidence after validation criteria are met

The label **Established** must not be available merely because a user completed every question.

## 11. Narrative archetype system

### 11.1 Governing rule

Archetypes summarize the shape of a five-dimensional profile; they do not replace the five scores. A pet may be close to two archetypes, and its archetype may change when behavior or evidence changes.

### 11.2 Provisional 16-profile library

H, M, and L describe prototype centers within the WildKind story system, not population classifications.

| Archetype | DD | SE | BS | BR | ER | Narrative essence |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|---|
| 🔥 Flame Vanguard | H | H | M | L | H | Moves quickly toward novelty and often acts before pausing |
| ❄️ Glacier Watcher | L | L | M | H | H | Calm, self-paced, predictable, and selective about engagement |
| 🌙 Moonshadow Ranger | H | L | L | M | H | Independent exploration with little need for social participation |
| 🍯 Sunmeadow Guardian | L | M | H | H | H | Warm, steady, familiar-routine oriented, and dependable in known settings |
| ⚡ Thundertrail Scout | H | H | M | M | M | Active, socially visible, and eager to investigate what happens next |
| ✨ Starlight Inventor | H | L | M | L | M | Quietly experimental and inclined to discover unconventional solutions |
| 🪨 Riverstone Companion | M | M | H | H | H | Affiliative, balanced, and easy to settle into shared routines |
| 🌿 Mosswood Keeper | L | L | H | H | M | Private, gentle, and deeply comfortable with familiar people and places |
| 🌅 Golden Harbor Host | M | H | H | H | H | Socially welcoming, cooperative, and steady in familiar gatherings |
| 🌪️ Wildwind Trickster | H | H | M | L | M | Fast-moving, inventive, and likely to repeat whatever produces excitement |
| 💧 Dewlight Comforter | L | M | H | M | L | Affection-oriented and responsive, with a need for gentle transitions |
| ⛈️ Stormglass Sentinel | M | L | L | H | L | Watchful, selective, and most comfortable when change is predictable |
| ❤️ Emberheart Ally | M | H | H | M | L | Highly engaged with companions and emotionally responsive to the setting |
| ☁️ Cloudpath Wanderer | H | M | L | L | H | Flexible, independent, and drawn toward discovery more than routine |
| 🌳 Willowshade Observer | M | L | M | H | M | Deliberate, orderly, and inclined to watch before participating |
| 🌌 Aurora Pathfinder | H | M | H | H | M | Curious and cooperative, balancing exploration with connection |

### 11.3 Provisional assignment method

Before empirical clusters exist:

1. Represent L, M, and H prototype centers as 20, 50, and 80.
2. Calculate squared distance from the pet's five available domain scores to every prototype.
3. Select the nearest prototype only if all five domain scores are available.
4. Calculate the square root of each distance. If the gap between the two closest distances is less than 10 points, show a blended result: “Primarily Aurora Pathfinder, with Riverstone Companion qualities.”
5. Label the result **provisional narrative match**.

`distance = Σ(domain score − prototype center)²`

This calculation is transparent but not a scientific discovery of natural types. After a sufficiently large sample exists, replace provisional centers with reproducible species-specific latent profiles or clusters and verify them in a separate sample.

### 11.4 Archetype description template

Each archetype page should contain:

1. One-sentence poetic introduction
2. Three observable behavioral tendencies
3. Conditions in which the profile may look different
4. Two likely enrichment preferences
5. One care consideration
6. One “do not assume” statement

Example:

> **Flame Vanguard:** Novelty often becomes an invitation to act. Pets near this profile tend to investigate quickly, join activity readily, and transition less easily when excitement is interrupted. They may benefit from safe exploration followed by a predictable settling routine. Do not assume that enthusiasm means every new animal, person, or environment is safe or welcome.

## 12. Recommendation engine

Recommendations must be generated from dimensions and context, not from the archetype name alone.

### 12.1 Dimension-level guidance library

| Pattern | Supportive care idea | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Higher DD | Offer voluntary novelty, scent/search activities, rotating safe enrichment, and choice-led exploration | Constant stimulation, forced exposure, or assuming curiosity removes risk |
| Lower DD | Introduce one change at a time, preserve retreat options, pair novelty with familiar cues | Flooding, carrying the animal toward novelty, or calling caution stubbornness |
| Higher SE | Provide appropriate social outlets and planned recovery periods | Assuming all people or animals are welcome |
| Lower SE | Protect uninterrupted rest, allow observation at a distance, use consent-based invitations | Repeated calling, forced greetings, or interpreting privacy as unhappiness |
| Higher BS | Use cooperative routines and familiar caregiver participation | Assuming affiliation guarantees tolerance of handling |
| Lower BS | Build predictable low-pressure interaction and reward voluntary proximity | Pursuing contact, restraint, or moral labels such as aloof or disloyal |
| Higher BR | Use predictable routines while still offering manageable variation | Overlooking boredom because the animal appears easy to manage |
| Lower BR | Use short activities, clear endings, accessible alternatives, and gradual transition cues | Punishment for difficulty disengaging or prolonged frustration exercises |
| Higher ER | Introduce manageable change while continuing to monitor subtle stress | Assuming low visible reaction means no stress or no need for support |
| Lower ER | Increase predictability, distance, retreat access, and recovery time | Repeated exposure intended to “toughen up” the animal |

### 12.2 Recommendation construction rule

Each report should select:

- One recommendation for the most strongly expressed domain
- One recommendation for the least strongly expressed domain
- One recommendation based on an interaction between two domains
- No more than three immediate actions, to prevent overload

Every action should include:

- **Try:** a specific low-risk activity
- **Start with:** a conservative duration or number of repetitions
- **Look for:** relaxed engagement or voluntary return
- **Pause if:** tension, avoidance, escalating frustration, or prolonged recovery appears

Durations must be presented as starting points, not universal requirements.

### 12.3 Example interaction rules

- High DD + low BR: enrichment with clear endings and accessible alternative activities
- High SE + low ER: small predictable social interactions with longer recovery time
- Low SE + high BS: prioritize a few trusted relationships over group socialization
- Low DD + low ER: introduce environmental changes gradually and preserve familiar resources
- High DD + high ER: offer varied voluntary exploration while maintaining normal safety controls
- High BS + low BR: use caregiver connection to support transitions, without reinforcing escalating demand behavior

### 12.4 Clinical and safety escalation

Do not issue personality-only advice when the owner reports:

- Sudden substantial behavior change
- Suspected pain or illness
- Self-injury or severe escape behavior
- Persistent inability to eat, sleep, eliminate, or rest normally
- Repeated defensive behavior that could injure a person or animal
- Severe distress when left alone

Use this message:

> This pattern may involve more than personality. A veterinarian should first rule out pain or medical causes. A qualified behavior professional can then help assess the behavior in context.

## 13. Result report specification

### 13.1 Page 1 — Profile summary

- Pet name, species, age/life stage, assessment date, and assessment version
- “Owner-Reported Snapshot” or “Enhanced Profile” badge
- Provisional archetype and one-sentence description
- Five-domain radar chart on a 0–100 assessment scale
- Coverage and context badges
- Clear statement that the result is continuous and context-dependent

### 13.2 Page 2 — Five dimensions

For every domain show:

- Score
- Source: owner, observed session, or both shown separately
- Coverage
- Two behaviors contributing to the score
- One condition that could affect interpretation
- No percentile until valid norms exist

### 13.3 Page 3 — Care plan

- Three immediate care or enrichment actions
- What relaxed engagement may look like
- Stop or adjustment signals
- One owner observation to record during the next month

### 13.4 Page 4 — Context and agreement

- Health and life-change context
- Owner/assessor comparison when applicable
- Neutral explanation of discrepancies
- Missing or unobservable areas

### 13.5 Page 5 — Tracking and references

- Suggested retest timing
- Previous-score comparison if available
- Version and norm group when applicable
- References supporting each domain's development
- Product limitation and welfare disclaimer

## 14. Retesting and longitudinal tracking

### 14.1 Default timing

- New adoption or major transition: wait until at least 90 days in a stable home when possible
- Routine tracking: approximately every three to six months
- Puppies, kittens, and adolescents: more frequent tracking may be offered, but interpret as development rather than fixed personality
- Sudden behavior change: veterinary review instead of immediate personality retesting

### 14.2 Pre-retest context check

Record changes in:

- Health, pain, mobility, sleep, and appetite
- Medication
- Home and household composition
- Daily schedule
- Training or enrichment
- Reproductive status when relevant
- Major frightening or stressful events
- Assessment mode and respondent identity

### 14.3 Change display

Show:

- Raw score at each date
- Coverage at each date
- Context flags at each date
- A shaded measurement-uncertainty region only after empirical error estimates exist
- Age-adjusted percentile only after species- and life-stage-specific norms exist

Do not call every score movement personality change. Use “observed score change” unless it exceeds a validated reliable-change threshold.

## 15. Community matching

WildKind scores may support discovery but must not certify compatibility.

### 15.1 Owner-selected goal

Ask the owner to choose:

- Similar-temperament community
- Calm companionship
- Active enrichment ideas
- Walking or outing partner
- Training and care discussion
- Potential pet introduction

### 15.2 Matching inputs

| Input | Suggested role |
|---|---|
| Owner's stated goal | Required filter |
| Species and known compatibility | Required filter |
| Location and availability | Practical filter |
| Size, age, health, and play style | Safety/context filter |
| Five WildKind dimensions | Recommendation signal |
| Archetype | Search and storytelling label only |

### 15.3 Safety wording

Never say “95% compatible” unless the percentage is clearly described as an app recommendation score rather than the probability of safe interaction.

Use:

> These profiles share similar activity and recovery patterns. Compatibility still depends on health, history, environment, introductions, and supervision.

## 17. Data model

### 17.1 Core entities

- `Pet`: species, birth date or life stage, sex, reproductive status if supplied, time in home, environment variables
- `Assessment`: version, mode, date, respondent, language, completion status
- `Response`: item version, raw response, keyed score, missing reason
- `ContextFlag`: category, severity, user note, date
- `ObservationSession`: location type, assessor, consent, task order, stop events
- `ObservationMetric`: task, metric, raw value, unit, coder, timestamp
- `DomainResult`: source, raw score, coverage, norm version, percentile if valid
- `ArchetypeMatch`: library version, first match, second match, distances
- `Recommendation`: rule version, triggering dimensions, displayed text
- `LongitudinalEvent`: health, environment, household, or care change

### 17.2 Versioning requirements

Store the exact versions of:

- Questionnaire wording
- Translation
- Scoring keys
- Observation protocol
- Archetype library
- Recommendation rules
- Normative reference population

Never silently recalculate an old report under new norms. If recalculation is offered, display both versions and explain the change.

### 17.3 Privacy minimums

- Exact home location must not be part of the personality assessment dataset.
- Video requires separate explicit consent and a retention period.
- Owners must be able to delete private assessments and revoke research use where applicable.
- Public community profiles should expose only owner-selected results.
- Sensitive notes about defensive or distress behavior should be private by default.

## 18. Scoring pseudocode

```text
for each assessment:
    verify eligibility and consent
    store all raw responses unchanged

    for each scored item:
        if response is NOT_OBSERVED:
            keyed_score = missing
        else if item is reverse_keyed:
            keyed_score = 4 - response
        else:
            keyed_score = response

    for each domain:
        available = keyed domain items that are not missing
        if count(available) < 4:
            domain_score = missing
            coverage = INSUFFICIENT
        else:
            domain_score = mean(available) * 25
            coverage = coverage_rule(domain, item_31)

    flags = derive_context_flags(items_26_to_32)

    if all five domain scores are available:
        for each provisional archetype:
            distance = sum((score - prototype_center)^2)
        first_match = smallest distance
        second_match = second-smallest distance
        if distances are nearly tied:
            display blended narrative match

    recommendations = select_dimension_rules(
        domain_scores,
        species,
        life_stage,
        context_flags,
        safety_flags
    )

    if clinical_or_safety_escalation:
        suppress playful risk interpretation
        display veterinary/behavior guidance

    render report with assessment and rule versions
```

## 19. Research and validation program

### 19.1 Phase A — Content validity

- Map every item to a defined construct and behavioral indicator.
- Obtain review from veterinary behavior, animal welfare, psychometrics, and species specialists.
- Conduct owner cognitive interviews to identify ambiguous wording.
- Review every task for welfare and feasibility.
- Create separate dog and cat codebooks.

### 19.2 Phase B — Pilot reliability

- Recruit sufficiently large and diverse dog and cat samples separately.
- Analyze missingness and opportunity bias.
- Test item distributions and redundancy.
- Estimate internal consistency without relying on coefficient alpha alone.
- Conduct test–retest assessment under stable conditions.
- Double-code at least 20% of observation videos.

### 19.3 Phase C — Structure and validity

- Use exploratory factor analysis in a development sample.
- Revise or remove weak and cross-loading items.
- Use confirmatory factor analysis in a separate sample.
- Examine convergent relationships with appropriate established species-specific measures.
- Test discriminant validity between conceptually different domains.
- Test predictive validity against future behavior in defined, low-risk contexts.
- Evaluate measurement invariance across language, age group, sex, and relevant environment groups.

### 19.4 Phase D — Norms and archetypes

- Build representative species-specific reference samples.
- Publish sample composition and norm date.
- Create life-stage norms only where sample sizes support them.
- Explore latent-profile or clustering solutions.
- Retain archetypes only if profiles reproduce in a holdout sample and remain interpretable.

### 19.5 Phase E — Practical outcome validation

Measure whether WildKind:

- Improves owner recognition of behavioral signals
- Encourages appropriate enrichment and consent-based interaction
- Reduces unrealistic expectations
- Supports more useful owner-to-owner discussions
- Avoids increasing forced socialization or unsafe introductions

### 19.6 Product evidence labels

| Product stage | Allowed wording |
|---|---|
| Concept | “Research-informed design” |
| Pilot | “Pilot assessment under evaluation” |
| Reliability established | Name the specific reliability evidence |
| Validity evidence established | Name the population, language, species, and form validated |
| Normed | Name the reference sample and norm version |

Never use “scientifically proven” as a blanket marketing phrase.

## 20. Reference foundation

The following sources support the development direction but do not automatically validate WildKind itself:

1. Gosling, S. D., & John, O. P. (1999). *Personality Dimensions in Nonhuman Animals: A Cross-Species Review.* The review found the strongest cross-species generality for Extraversion, Neuroticism, and Agreeableness, with less generality for Openness and a separate Conscientiousness dimension only in chimpanzees. [Article](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1111/1467-8721.00017)
2. Litchfield, C. A., et al. (2017). *The ‘Feline Five’: An exploration of personality in pet cats.* The cat factors were Neuroticism, Extraversion, Dominance, Impulsiveness, and Agreeableness. [Open-access article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5568325/)
3. Hsu, Y., & Serpell, J. A. (2003). *Development and validation of a questionnaire for measuring behavior and temperament traits in pet dogs.* The development process used situational owner-report items and factor analysis in a large dog sample. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14621216/)
4. University of Pennsylvania. *About the C-BARQ.* C-BARQ is primarily described as a standardized evaluation of canine behavioral problems, so WildKind should not portray it as a generic cross-species Big Five test. [Official C-BARQ page](https://vetapps.vet.upenn.edu/cbarq/about.cfm)
5. Kubinyi, E., et al. (2020). *Individual and group level personality change across the lifespan in dogs.* This supports a dynamic perspective while also illustrating why age claims must be trait- and population-specific. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33057125/)

## 21. Final report disclaimer

> WildKind describes recurring behavioral tendencies based on recent owner observations and, when applicable, a structured observation session. Results can be affected by health, pain, learning history, environment, life stage, and the situations available for observation. WildKind is not a veterinary diagnosis, a prediction of aggression, or a guarantee that animals or people can interact safely. Sudden or concerning behavior changes should be discussed with a veterinarian or qualified behavior professional.
